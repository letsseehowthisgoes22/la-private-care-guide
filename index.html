How to vet private behavioral health providers in Los Angeles

Use this guide when your family, attorney or family office is comparing Los Angeles County providers for a planned behavioral health referral. It covers how to verify credentials, ask about the actual care arrangement and record the decision without circulating private health details.

Where to start

Before committing, verify the clinician or program under its legal name, confirm who will actually provide care, and get fees, appointment availability and the handoff plan in writing. Ask the participating family member what matters to them and how they want others involved.

Go to the checklist

Check the clinician, the facility and the exact address

Ask intake for the name of the treating professional, the license type and the license number. For clinicians regulated by California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences, use its license verification page to reach the state record and review the status and any public documents. [1] For a different profession, ask which licensing board maintains its record and verify there.

For a facility, ask for its legal name, address, program type and license or certification number, if applicable. California DHCS publishes resources for certain licensed mental health facilities and separate directories for substance use disorder programs. Start with the listing for the actual service and match the street address to the one quoted by intake. [2] [3]

  • Outpatient practiceRecord the names and credentials of the people who will meet with the patient. Ask who handles follow-ups or coverage when the assigned clinician is away.
  • Residential optionGet the site address and program category in writing. Ask which services are delivered at the location and which are arranged through a separate provider.
  • County optionsLos Angeles County organizes services geographically. West LA Service Area 5 includes Beverly Hills, Malibu, Santa Monica and other Westside communities. Its resource page is a useful starting point for county-operated and contracted options. [4]

Compare two providers using the same questions

Call each candidate with the same practical questions and write down the name of the person who answered and the date. Separate what intake has confirmed from what the clinical team still needs to assess. A family lawyer or office manager can organize the comparison while the participant and treating professionals discuss care choices.

Plan for the appointment the family member will actually attend. Ask for the full address and expected frequency before picking a location. For virtual visits, give the provider the state where the participant expects to be and confirm whether appointments can continue during travel.

  • AvailabilityHow soon is the assessment, how soon could regular appointments begin, and who calls back if the first opening moves?
  • Total costWhat does the initial fee cover? Ask for ongoing rates, separately billed professionals, cancellation terms and any deposit or refund terms in writing. Confirm coverage with the plan if using insurance.
  • How the work endsWho discusses progress with the participant, how will the plan be reviewed, and who arranges a referral if this provider cannot meet the person's needs?

Create a referral brief the family office can act on

Keep the office record focused on decisions the office actually needs to support. A useful record can be one page with contact names, quotes, practical constraints and open tasks. Give the provider and participating family member room to discuss what can be shared and through which channel. Use the provider’s agreed secure channel for health information.

  • Participant's prioritiesAsk which constraints should drive the shortlist, such as appointment hours, location, language, accessibility and who they want involved.
  • Referral relationshipsAsk advisors whether they receive compensation for a referral or have an ownership or business relationship with the provider. Record the answer next to the quoted fee and the other options considered.
  • First actionWrite down who will confirm the appointment, the date they will report back and which fallback contact they will use if the slot falls through.

Common questions

How do I verify a California therapist’s license?

Ask for their full professional name, license type and number. For a clinician overseen by the Board of Behavioral Sciences, follow the state search link on its verification page and inspect the active status and any public documents. [1]

Where can I check a residential behavioral health program near Los Angeles?

Identify the service category and exact street address first. California DHCS provides a facilities page for certain mental health settings and a separate directory for substance use treatment programs. Ask which agency oversees the specific service you are comparing. [2] [3]

What should a family office send on its first inquiry?

Share your role, the broad purpose of the call, the general area, scheduling constraints and your business contact information. Arrange a secure channel directly with the provider before sharing care records.

Where can I get help if the situation cannot wait for a planned referral?

Call 911 for immediate danger or a medical emergency. The Los Angeles County Help Line for Mental Health and Substance Use Services is available 24/7 at 800-854-7771. [5]
